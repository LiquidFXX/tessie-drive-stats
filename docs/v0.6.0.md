# v0.6.0 — Charging Economics

Tessie Drive Stats v0.6.0 adds **Charging Economics**: local analytics that combine Tessie charging history with real drive energy usage to estimate what the vehicle costs to operate.

## What is new

The release adds last-charge, day/week/month/year, and recorded-lifetime charging economics including:

- charging efficiency and charging loss
- energy-weighted average charging cost per kWh
- charging-cost coverage so missing Tessie costs are visible instead of silently treated as free charging
- estimated driving cost and estimated cost per driven mile
- last-charge and last-Supercharger cost per kWh
- recorded-lifetime economics using the overlapping portion of Tessie charging and driving history
- recorded-lifetime Supercharger vs explicitly non-Supercharger average cost per kWh
- recorded-lifetime Supercharger cost premium

## Charging efficiency

Tessie defines charging efficiency as:

```text
energy added to battery / energy used by charger × 100
```

The integration follows that definition. Aggregate efficiency includes only charging sessions where Tessie reported both `energy_added` and a positive `energy_used`, so partial records do not skew the result.

Charging loss is reported as:

```text
100 - charging efficiency
```

Tessie measurements are estimates, so unusual source data can theoretically produce values outside the range a perfect physical meter would show. Tessie Drive Stats does not silently clamp those measurements.

## Average charging cost per kWh

Average charging cost per kWh is energy-weighted:

```text
sum(recorded cost) / sum(charger-side energy used)
```

Only sessions containing both a Tessie cost and positive charger-side energy are included. A missing cost is **not** assumed to mean free charging.

## Charging cost coverage

Because Tessie charging costs can be configured per location or through a default electric rate, some historical sessions may not have a cost. Charging Economics therefore exposes cost-coverage sensors.

Coverage is the percentage of charger-side energy belonging to sessions that have a recorded cost:

```text
energy with cost / all reported charger energy × 100
```

A low coverage percentage means cost-per-kWh and driving-cost estimates may underrepresent locations where a cost has not been configured in Tessie.

## Estimated driving cost

Tessie documents driving costs as using average charging cost per kWh against energy later spent on the road. Tessie Drive Stats follows the same concept:

```text
estimated driving cost = drive energy used × average charging cost per kWh
```

Estimated cost per mile uses actual recorded driving distance and drive energy:

```text
estimated cost per mile = estimated driving cost / driven miles
```

These are estimates, not accounting-grade billing totals. Energy can be charged in one period and driven in another, and missing charging-cost records reduce cost coverage.

## Recorded-lifetime economics

Charging and driving history can begin on different dates. To avoid dividing a long charging history by a shorter driving history (or vice versa), recorded-lifetime driving-cost sensors begin at the **later** of the earliest available charge and drive timestamps.

The entity `recorded_lifetime_economics_since` exposes that common coverage date.

Recorded-lifetime charging efficiency and charging-cost coverage still summarize all available Tessie charge history.

## Supercharger comparison

The privacy-minimized lifetime cache intentionally removes historical location names and coordinates. For that reason, v0.6.0 does **not** guess that every non-Supercharger session occurred at Home.

Instead it compares sessions explicitly classified by Tessie as:

- Supercharger
- non-Supercharger

Sessions without an explicit `is_supercharger` classification are excluded from that split.

`recorded_lifetime_supercharger_cost_premium` is calculated as the percentage difference between the energy-weighted Supercharger rate and the energy-weighted explicitly non-Supercharger rate.

## API load and privacy

Charging Economics adds **no additional Tessie API endpoint**. It reuses:

- current-year charge and drive history already fetched by the coordinator
- the privacy-minimized recorded-lifetime charge and drive cache introduced in v0.4.0

No new historical address, saved-location, GPS, or route data is persisted for these calculations.

## Compatibility

v0.6.0 adds new entities without changing the existing VIN-based unique IDs from earlier versions. Existing v0.5 and older entities should retain their registry identities after upgrade.
